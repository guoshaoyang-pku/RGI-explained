\section{Limits and conclusion}

The common-checkpoint experiment supports a concrete local explanation and identifies its limits.  Frozen difficulty dominates raw batch fluctuation at sufficiently small registered rates.  Negative parameter work is the main source of learning-response variation, while positive logit variance offsets part of it.  The full-model remainder contains network curvature that is removed by the affine output-head control; large displacements also invalidate the softmax quadratic approximation.  These are separately measured terms, not an attribution inferred from correlated losses.

The strongest desired conclusion remains obstructed.  Low-rate SGD/HB endpoint displacements nearly align, but accumulated response closure can fail and their small token-wise gap is not a nonzero constant offset.  Even the terminal smaller-rate SGD/HB full-window errors exceed the fixed 10\% gate.  Adam and the head-only control pass in that setting.  We do not shrink the rate further in this revision or relabel failed settings as validation of a universal coefficient.  The exact decomposition and the alignment bound organize the evidence; neither makes its approximation gates pass automatically.

The study uses Pythia-70M late-Pile-checkpoint continuations on a reused FineWeb pool, three permutations rather than independent datasets, reset optimizer states, and fixed 512-update rather than matched effective-progress horizons.  The second and third rate protocols are explicitly adaptive.  Initial Adam curvature matching is not a match of direction or learning speed.  All finite-response terms use observed displacements.  The independent checker reconstructs recorded arithmetic and saved fixtures; it does not independently replay every training trajectory.  The original RGI paper's larger, from-scratch pretraining setting may consequently occupy a different regime.

The result is a falsifiable mechanism study: it locates a shared batch difficulty, a method-dependent first-order response, two distinct curvature sources, and the measured terms that prevent a stronger constant-gap inference.  A future explanation of strong RGI must additionally make the centered paired response small on the relevant token support, and verify that condition on an untouched window or independent data.
