\section{A finite-update decomposition}

Consider one prefix--target pair \((x,y)\), with logits \(z_0(x)\) at the frozen start \(\theta_0\), probabilities \(p_0=\operatorname{softmax}(z_0)\), and logits \(z_t\) after the observed continuation.  Write \(\Delta z=z_t-z_0\), \(p_t=\operatorname{softmax}(z_t)\), and \(e_y\) for the one-hot target.  For a batch, all quantities below are averaged over the same target positions.

The frozen difficulty is
\begin{equation}
  D(B)=\operatorname{mean}_{(x,y)\in B}\left[-\log p_0(y\mid x)\right].
  \label{eq:difficulty}
\end{equation}
The exact change in cross-entropy has a useful logit-space identity,
\begin{align}
  L_t(B)-D(B)&=W(B)+Q(B),\\
  W(B)&=\operatorname{mean}\left[(p_0-e_y)^\top\Delta z\right],\\
  Q(B)&=\operatorname{mean}\left[\KL(p_0\Vert p_t)\right]\geq 0.
  \label{eq:exact}
\end{align}
The equality follows from the log-partition identity and is used as an arithmetic check.  It is not a forecast because it uses the observed final logits.

To connect the loss change to parameter motion, recompute the batch gradient at \(\theta_0\) and define
\begin{equation}
  A(B)=\nabla_\theta\ell_{\theta_0}(B)^\top(\theta_t-\theta_0),
  \qquad R(B)=W(B)-A(B).
  \label{eq:work}
\end{equation}
The exact change can then be written \(L_t-D=A+R+Q\).  The residual \(R\) includes the nonlinearity of the parameter-to-logit map along the observed displacement, as well as any replay or numerical error; we do not identify it with a single unmeasured Hessian term.

The second-order logit approximation to the convex log-partition term is
\begin{equation}
  Q_2(B)=\frac12\operatorname{mean}_{(x,y)\in B}\left[\operatorname{Var}_{v\sim p_0(\cdot\mid x)}\left(\Delta z_v(x)\right)\right].
  \label{eq:q2}
\end{equation}
Our primary local closure is therefore \(L_t-D\approx A+Q_2\).  Both \(A\) and \(Q_2\) use the displacement that actually occurred: \(A\) uses \(\theta_t-\theta_0\), and \(Q_2\) uses \(\Delta z\).  The test explains a realized finite response; it does not predict a future response from a frozen gradient alone.

We retain the measured remainder as the fourth primary term:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{L_t(B)=D(B)+A_t(B)+Q_{2,t}(B)+\varepsilon_t(B)},
  \qquad \varepsilon_t=R_t+(Q_t-Q_{2,t}).
  \label{eq:four}
\end{equation}
Adding the observed remainder gives an identity; the substantive approximation is that the remainder is small on the response scale \(L-D\).  Its magnitude and its two components are tested rather than assumed.

\paragraph{Two sources of local curvature.}
Let \(J_0=D_\theta z(\theta_0)\), \(r=p_0-e_y\), and \(C_0=\operatorname{diag}(p_0)-p_0p_0^\top\).  The exact checkpoint Hessian is
\begin{equation}
  H_\ell=J_0^\top C_0J_0+\sum_v r_v H_{z_v}.
  \label{eq:hessian}
\end{equation}
For a small parameter displacement \(\delta\), the leading variance term is
\(Q_2=\tfrac12\delta^\top J_0^\top C_0J_0\delta+O(\|\delta\|^3)\),
while \(R=\tfrac12\delta^\top(\sum_v r_vH_{z_v})\delta+O(\|\delta\|^3)\).
The full loss curvature therefore contains both probability curvature and network curvature.  In particular, \(\varepsilon\) is generally second order in parameter motion, even though \(Q-Q_2\) is third order in the local logit displacement under smoothness and bounded derivatives.  We do not equate \(Q_2\) with the full parameter-Hessian sharpness.

An output-head-only continuation gives an exact control.  Frozen hidden states make logits affine in the changed output matrix, so \(W=A\), \(R=0\), and \(\varepsilon=Q-Q_2\) up to numerical precision.  This separates network nonlinearity from the log-partition approximation without changing the initial predictive function.

\paragraph{A paired criterion for strong RGI.}
Let \(P\) subtract the mean over a fixed set of prefix--target pairs.  Two branches from the same checkpoint satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\ell=\Delta A+\Delta Q_2+\Delta\varepsilon,
  \qquad
  P\Delta\ell=P(\Delta A+\Delta Q_2+\Delta\varepsilon).
  \label{eq:paired}
\end{equation}
The shared \(D\) cancels.  Thus a small raw-loss discrepancy can be dominated by frozen difficulty while a nonzero constant offset still fails: that stronger property requires the centered response in Eq.~\eqref{eq:paired} to be small relative to its mean.  This is a measurable criterion, rather than a consequence of the coefficient one in Eq.~\eqref{eq:four}.

For a fixed teacher distribution \(q(\cdot\mid x)\), the same algebra replaces \(e_y\) by \(q\).  With \(p_t=\operatorname{softmax}(z_q+v)\), the teacher-forced hard-target loss decomposes as
\begin{equation}
  \ell=S+C+E,\qquad S=-\log q_y,\quad C=(q-e_y)^\top v,\quad E=\KL(q\Vert p_t).
  \label{eq:teacher}
\end{equation}
Under a target drawn from \(q\), \(\mathbb{E}_q[C\mid x]=0\), and the population cross-entropy is \(H(q)+E\).  We use this controlled population objective to separate label sampling from the natural-text case, where the unknown conditional distribution is not observed.
