\section{Introduction}

Language-model loss reflects both the batch that arrives and the motion of the predictor.  The same difficult targets can raise several models' losses together, while the training method changes how their predictions move.  A useful explanation should locate both contributions on the same teacher-forced targets and verify which dominates at the scale being measured.

Relative Generalization Invariance (RGI)~\citep{zhang2026relativegeneralizationinvariancellm} asks why loss differences across some training settings remain approximately preserved across validation tokens.  A complementary study~\citep{lee2026journeyedgestability} finds near-overlapping low-rate loss and sharpness traces for normalized linear gradient filters.  Neither scalar trace agreement nor a stylized construction identifies which sample-conditioned terms dominate a real Transformer continuation.  High raw-loss correlation also need not imply a constant token-wise gap.

We make the mechanism measurable.  All branches start from the same late Pythia-70M checkpoint, reset optimizer state, re-warm up, and see the same prescribed batch sequence.  The frozen checkpoint gives difficulty \(D\); the actual parameter displacement gives frozen-gradient work \(A\); and the full-vocabulary logit displacement gives nonnegative variance \(Q_2\).  We measure the remainder \(\varepsilon\) rather than assume it negligible.  The central table and four-panel curves show these terms separately, without fitted slopes, intercepts, or time warps.

Three questions are distinct: whether \(D\) dominates raw arriving-loss variation, whether \(A+Q_2\) closes \(L-D\), and whether a paired gap is constant across tokens after \(D\) cancels.  We test each with its own prespecified denominator and gate, retaining failed settings and evaluating a fixed held-out probe alongside the arriving stream.  Our output-head-only control makes logits affine in the updated parameters and removes network nonlinearity exactly.  A same-stream SGD/momentum identity separates gradient feedback from optimizer memory and warmup.

The claims are local.  Rate extensions are disclosed as adaptive follow-ups to failed long-displacement approximations, not an untouched confirmation.  These finite Pythia-70M continuations are not reproductions of the original 124M and 0.7B pretraining runs or a universal Transformer law.
