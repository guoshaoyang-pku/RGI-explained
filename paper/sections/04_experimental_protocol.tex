\section{Common late-checkpoint protocol}

\paragraph{Common model and targets.}
All six settings load the official Pythia-70M~\citep{biderman2023pythiasuiteanalyzinglarge} step-143,000 checkpoint, revision \texttt{de3e4e2d...}, with identical 70,426,624 parameters and initial forward computation.  The checkpoint is late Pile pretraining; the continuation distribution is FineWeb~\citep{penedo2024finewebdatasetsdecantingweb}.  We do not assume equilibrium on the latter.  We reuse a registered 4,672-document FineWeb sample-10BT pool, split 4,096/64/512 into training, calibration, and evaluation.  Each document contributes one 256-token span.  Teacher-forced scoring uses 223 next-token targets at positions 33--255 and the full 50,304-token Pythia vocabulary.  Selection used no model scores, but the pool was used by previous diagnostics; it is not a fresh held-out confirmation.

\paragraph{Paired restarts.}
Each setting runs 512 batch-eight updates with zero initial optimizer state and a 32-update linear warmup followed by a constant rate.  Three registered permutations use seeds 202610081--202610083.  They are paired data-order interventions, not independent datasets.  Arriving-batch measurements precede its update.  At the shared checkpoint, \(A=Q_2=\varepsilon=0\).  A separate 32-document held-out probe is scored at steps 0, 16, 32, 64, 128, and every 64 steps through 512; its frozen \(D\) is constant in time.

The six settings are full-model SGD and Heavy Ball at two effective rates, reset Adam~\citep{kingma2017adammethodstochasticoptimization}, and output-head-only SGD at the smaller rate.  Heavy Ball uses \(\beta=0.9\), no dampening, no Nesterov update, and raw SGD rate \((1-\beta)h\), giving the same dc-normalized rate \(h\) as SGD.  No setting uses weight decay or dropout.  Head-only training preserves the initial predictive function and freezes all parameters except the output matrix.

Adam uses betas \((0.9,0.95)\), \(\epsilon=10^{-8}\), and a scalar rate calibrated before training on the first 16 calibration documents.  A reset one-step update is scaled to match the smaller-rate SGD full-vocabulary \(Q_2\), with at most two square-root ratio corrections.  This matches an initial functional step scale, not dc gain, direction, or training progress.  Evaluation scores do not enter calibration.

\paragraph{Rate protocols and adaptive chronology.}
The first registered rates were \(h=3\times10^{-4}\) and \(1.2\times10^{-3}\).  After their smaller-rate replica-zero SGD/momentum closure failed, a second protocol fixed \(3\times10^{-6}\) and \(3\times10^{-5}\).  Their replica-zero closures also failed, so a terminal protocol fixed \(3\times10^{-8}\) and \(3\times10^{-7}\) before its measurements.  Every protocol runs all six settings and all three permutations; all results are retained.  This is an explicitly adaptive rate study.  Smaller and larger identify registered rates within each protocol, not measured stability regimes.  Fixed 512-update comparisons do not establish a fixed-effective-progress asymptotic limit.

\paragraph{Measurements, precision, and gates.}
Parameters, gradients, logits, and reductions use FP64; TF32 is disabled.  We recompute the frozen batch gradient for \(A\), and calculate \(W,Q,Q_2\) over the complete vocabulary.  Batch-level means contain all four primary terms; document records contain \(L,D,W,Q,Q_2\).  At step 512 a full-model JVP supplies token-level \(A\), checked against the independent parameter-gradient dot product.  These observed finite displacements explain realized responses rather than forecast unknown updates.

The frozen prediction fraction is \(1-\sum_t(L_t-D_t)^2/\sum_t(L_t-\bar L)^2\), with a 90\% gate.  Response closure uses \(\RMS(\varepsilon)/\RMS(L-D)\), with a 10\% gate; paired closure uses \(\RMS(\Delta\varepsilon)/\RMS(\Delta L)\).  Token constant-gap testing uses \(\operatorname{std}(\Delta\ell)/|\operatorname{mean}(\Delta\ell)|\) at 10\%, with near-zero means flagged separately.  Near \(0{:}128\), far \(384{:}512\), full \(0{:}512\), and nonoverlapping 64-step blocks are fixed in advance.  Signed covariance shares can be negative because terms cancel and are descriptive rather than independent causal effects.  The independent checker reconstructs saved full-vocabulary fixtures, identities, schedules, and hashes; it does not independently replay every network gradient or training step.
