Relative Generalization Invariance (RGI) describes approximately sample-independent loss differences across language-model training settings.  From a shared late Pythia checkpoint, we derive and measure $L=D+A+Q_2+\varepsilon$.  $D$ is shared frozen-checkpoint batch cross-entropy, $A$ is the linear response to history-dependent parameter motion, $Q_2$ is half the frozen-probability-weighted variance of logit displacement, and $\varepsilon$ is the measured remainder.  Difficulty dominates raw fluctuations; $A+Q_2$ captures setting-dependent response when the remainder is small.  At the terminal scale, this closure holds accurately for calibrated Adam and head-only training, while network curvature remains material for SGD and momentum.  The decomposition explains shared fluctuations and smaller gaps, but a constant token-wise gap additionally requires the paired response to be nearly sample independent.
