Title: Beyond Relative Generalization Invariance: Shared Difficulty and Local Learning Response in Language Model Training
Authors: Shaoyang Guo, Ziming Liu

Abstract:
Relative Generalization Invariance describes approximately preserved token-wise loss differences across some language-model training settings.  We locate the terms behind loss similarity using a common late checkpoint and teacher-forced continuations.  The measured decomposition is L=D+A+Q_2+epsilon: frozen batch difficulty, frozen-gradient work on actual parameter motion, full-vocabulary logit variance, and a checked remainder.  Six settings, including normalized momentum, Adam, and output-head-only training, restart from Pythia-70M step 143000 with a shared stream and 32-step warmup.  Three paired permutations are evaluated under three explicitly adaptive rate protocols, retaining 54 completed trajectories.  At the terminal scale, shared difficulty explains over 99.9926% of raw arriving-loss variation.  Adam and the affine head-only control pass the 10% response gate, while full-model SGD/momentum still fail at 10.51-15.21%, predominantly through network nonlinearity.  The head-only intervention removes that component to numerical precision, and larger-rate settings expose softmax approximation failure.  An exact same-stream momentum identity connects optimizer memory to low-step alignment.  Nevertheless, endpoint displacement cosine above 0.9997 does not produce a constant nonzero token-loss gap.  The results identify measured local contributions and substantive boundaries of a universal RGI explanation, rather than infer one from raw-loss correlation.
